                                                                  A BSTRACT
Vietnamese e-commerce review platforms contain both legitimate reviews and several types of spam reviews. This paper presents a controlled
empirical study of Vietnamese spam-review classification using the ViSpamReviews v2 dataset and PhoBERT. First, we reproduce a single-task
PhoBERT baseline under a fixed training protocol. Second, we propose a lightweight multi-task architecture with a shared PhoBERT encoder and
two classification heads: a binary spam detector and a three-class spam-type classifier. Third, we evaluate generalization under a product-disjoint
split, in which reviews from the same product are not shared across train, development, and test sets. On the official released split, the reproduced
PhoBERT baseline obtains 76.69% Macro-F1 on the four-class task, while the multi-task model obtains 77.42%. On the more demanding
product-disjoint split, the multi-task model obtains 90.96% accuracy and 76.73% Macro-F1. The results show a small but consistent benefit from
multi-task supervision and a limited robustness gap between random and product-disjoint evaluation. We also report unsuccessful alternatives,
including weighted loss and rule augmentation, to make the experimental conclusions transparent. Index Terms—Vietnamese NLP, spam review
detection, PhoBERT, multi-task learning, e-commerce, robustness, product-disjoint evaluation.
